Country Music History

Origins

At a glance

Country music traces its roots back to the 1800’s along with similar genres like bluegrass. In the 18th and 19th centuries, many European migrants moved to the United States, eventually settling in Appalachia. Over the course of the next couple hundred years, Appalachian folk began to mix as a combination of traditional European folk songs, particularly ones from the British Isles. This is why instruments like the banjo, fiddle, and mandolin are common country and bluegrass instruments.


In the 1920’s, when record production and radio were becoming more popular, country music became what it eventually would be today. Widely considered to be the first commercially recorded country song with lyrics was Fiddlin' John Carson with "The Little Old Log Cabin in the Lane." Further down the road, The Bristol sessions in 1927 are recognized by the US government as the birth of country music, where popular artists like Jimmie Rodgers and the Carter Family recorded their music. Along with this, the Grand Ole Opry had its start in 1925 in Nashville, which was the largest country music radio show.

Jimmie Rodgers

Jimmie Rodgers basically invented the blueprint for the modern country music star. Known as the "Father of Country Music," he was a former railroad worker who turned to performing full-time after getting sick with tuberculosis, eventually getting discovered at the historic 1927 Bristol sessions by talent scout Ralph Peer. What made him a massive star was his totally unique sound: he mixed traditional Southern folk with early jazz, work chants, and African American blues, tying it all together with his signature guitar runs and famous "blue yodel". Over a wildly intense five-year career, he cut 110 tracks—including his breakout hit "Blue Yodel (T for Texas)" and a legendary collaboration with jazz icon Louis Armstrong—before tragically dying from a lung hemorrhage at just 35 years old. By shifting the spotlight away from old-time fiddle bands and putting it on the solo storyteller, Rodgers deeply shaped the DNA of American music and inspired generations of future legends like Johnny Cash and Merle Haggard.

The Carter Family

The Carter Family, known as the First Family of Country Music, was a foundational musical trio consisting of A. P. Carter, his wife Sara Dougherty Carter, and Sara's cousin Maybelle Addington Carter. Discovered during the famous 1927 Bristol Sessions, the group revolutionized country music over two decades of commercial success by pioneering vocal harmonies, collecting and arranging folk standards, and introducing Maybelle's influential thumb-brush guitar technique known as the Carter lick. Although they recorded over three hundred songs—including classics like Keep on the Sunny Side and Wildwood Flower—and expanded their audience through high-powered radio broadcasts, the trio never achieved massive crossover financial wealth and frequently returned to their Virginia home before disbanding in 1943. Inducted into the Country Music Hall of Fame in 1970, the group established a lasting legacy that reshaped American roots and country music.

"Can the Circle Be Unbroken"

Fidlin' John Carson

Fiddlin' John Carson was an influential old-time mountain musician who played a pivotal role in commercializing country music during the 1920s. After working various jobs including farming, rail work, and moonshining, Carson gained regional fame at Atlanta fiddlers' conventions before performing on WSB radio in 1922 and becoming a national radio personality. In June 1923, he recorded songs like The Little Old Log Cabin in the Lane for OKeh Records, creating an unexpected hit that demonstrated the massive commercial potential of rural southern music and launched a recording career of roughly 165 tracks. Often performing alongside his daughter Rosa Lee, who gained renown as Moonshine Kate, Carson blended traditional fiddle tunes with ballads until his music career waned in the 1930s, after which he spent his final years working as a state capitol elevator operator in Georgia.

"The Little Old Log Cabin in the Lane"
